## 2. Official DSA Category Field Definitions

The official EU DSA Transparency Database schema defines content violation categories across four distinct columns in daily dumps and complete aggregates:

| Field Name | Physical Data Type | Role in Schema Architecture | Official Regulatory Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `category` | String (`string`) | **Primary Category** (Categorical Grouping Column) | The mandatory primary statutory or contractual category of illegal content or terms-of-service incompatibility justifying the moderation notice under DSA Article 17. |
| `category_addition` | Array of Strings (Raw) | **Secondary Categories** (Multi-label Array) | Optional array allowing platforms to submit additional violation categories that also apply to the statement of reasons when a single piece of content violates multiple policies. |
| `STATEMENT_CATEGORY_*` | 14 Booleans (`bool not null`) | **Horizontally Exploded Flags** (from `category_addition`) | Pre-computed boolean flags generated by `dsa-tdb` during ETL by horizontally exploding the `category_addition` array against the 14 legacy category names. |
| `category_specification` | Array of Strings (Raw) | **Subcategories / Granular Keywords** | Optional array specifying the exact sub-ground or violative behavior (e.g., `KEYWORD_HATE_SPEECH`, `KEYWORD_CHILD_SEXUAL_ABUSE_MATERIAL`). |
| `KEYWORD_*` | 44 Booleans (`bool not null`) | **Horizontally Exploded Flags** (from `category_specification`) | Pre-computed boolean flags generated by `dsa-tdb` by horizontally exploding `category_specification`. |

## 5. Relationship Between `category` and Category Flags

1. **Source Decoupling**:
   - `category` stores the **primary** classification assigned to the SoR.
   - `STATEMENT_CATEGORY_*` flags store exploded **secondary** classifications from `category_addition`.
2. **Flag Disconnect**:
   - When `category == 'STATEMENT_CATEGORY_ILLEGAL_OR_HARMFUL_SPEECH'`, the flag `STATEMENT_CATEGORY_ILLEGAL_OR_HARMFUL_SPEECH` remains `False` because the primary category is not duplicated into `category_addition`.

## 9. Automation Field Official Semantics & Sanity Check

### Official Statutory Semantics (Article 17(2)(c) DSA):
Article 17(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act) requires providers of hosting services to disclose:
> *"where applicable, information on the use made of automated means in taking the decision, including whether the decision was taken in respect of content detected or identified using automated means."*

In the Commission's DSA Transparency Database Data Dictionary and `dsa-tdb` specification:
- **`automated_detection`** measures whether automated means were used to detect or identify the content (`Yes` / `No`).
- **`automated_decision`** measures the agency of automated means in **taking the moderation decision** itself (distinct from detection):

| Official Enum | Official Meaning (DSA Art. 17(2)(c) & TDB Spec) | Automated Means Participated in Decision? | Human Involvement Explicitly Implied? |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| `AUTOMATED_DECISION_FULLY` | The decision was taken **exclusively by automated means** without human intervention. | **Yes** (Exclusively) | **No** (Zero human review) |
| `AUTOMATED_DECISION_PARTIALLY` | The decision was taken **using automated means**, but with human involvement (e.g., human review assisted by automated tools or automated recommendation confirmed by a human reviewer). | **Yes** (Contributed) | **Yes** (Human-in-the-loop) |
| `AUTOMATED_DECISION_NOT_AUTOMATED` | Automated means were **not used in taking the decision**; the decision was made entirely by a human reviewer. | **No** | **Yes** (Sole human agency) |

### Semantic Validity of "Decision with Any Automation":
- Because the official definition of `AUTOMATED_DECISION_PARTIALLY` establishes that automated means participated in making the decision, it is semantically valid to define a consolidated metric capturing any algorithmic participation in the decision stage.
- **Metric Naming**: To maintain absolute precision, this metric is designated **"Decision with Any Automation Rate"** (`AUTOMATED_DECISION_FULLY` + `AUTOMATED_DECISION_PARTIALLY`), strictly distinguishing it from the **"Fully Automated Decision Rate"** (`AUTOMATED_DECISION_FULLY`).
- Both metrics will be reported side-by-side in Phase 5.

### B. Enforcement Reporting Methodology:

1. **Authoritative Output: Action Prevalence (Granular Multi-Label Output)**:
   The detailed multi-label **Action Prevalence Table** across the 14 normalized actions is the **authoritative Phase 5 enforcement output**.
   For each granular action:
   $$\text{Prevalence}(\text{Action}) = \frac{\sum (\text{count where action is present})}{\sum (\text{all SoRs in the platform/category slice})}$$
   These percentages are independent and are **not expected to sum to 100%** because an SoR can involve multiple concurrent actions (e.g., content removal alongside service suspension or age restriction). This detailed prevalence table provides the authoritative, objective record of platform enforcement behavior without imposing artificial exclusivity.

2. **Exclusive High-Level Channel: OPTIONAL / DEFERRED**:
   The construction of a mutually exclusive high-level enforcement channel (e.g., assigning each SoR to a single primary bucket such as Account Sanction vs. Content Removal vs. Other Visibility vs. Provision/Monetary) is classified as **OPTIONAL / DEFERRED** and is **not required for Phase 5 implementation**.
   - **Rationale**: Assigning an SoR to a single channel requires an analyst-defined priority hierarchy that is not inherent in the DSA regulatory schema.
   - **Governance**: No arbitrary priority hierarchy should enter the core processed benchmark unless explicitly approved later. A mutually exclusive hierarchy will only be constructed if a specific visualization in Phase 6 or Phase 7 clearly benefits from it, and the explicit priority rule will be formally documented and reviewed at that time.
   - **Summary**:
     - Phase 5 authoritative enforcement output = granular multi-label action prevalence
     - Exclusive high-level channel = optional / deferred, not required
     - No arbitrary priority hierarchy enters the core processed benchmark without explicit later approval

- **Methodological Rule**: To avoid severe structural classification artifacts, Phase 5 primary benchmarks operate strictly on the 11-month harmonized era (`2025-07-01` through `2026-05-31`).

## 12. Explicit Proposed Methodological Rules for Phase 5

The following 12 rules are proposed for Phase 5 implementation:

### Rule 1: Temporal Window
All primary quantitative benchmark metrics must be calculated strictly on the population:
```sql
created_at >= '2025-07-01' AND created_at <= '2026-05-31'
```
*(Defined by SoR submission timestamp `created_at`, excluding legacy partitions).*

### Rule 2: Platform Filter
The primary benchmark filters strictly for the three core services:
```sql
platform_name IN ('TikTok', 'YouTube', 'Instagram')
```

### Rule 3: Authoritative Category Column
All category aggregations must use the string column **`category`** directly. Pre-computed boolean flags `STATEMENT_CATEGORY_*` must NOT be used.

### Rule 4: Volume Weighting
All rate calculations must be weighted by the aggregate record volume:
$$\text{Rate} = \frac{\sum (\text{count} \times \text{Flag})}{\sum \text{count}}$$

### Rule 5: Independent Category Computations
Category-specific metrics are calculated independently on each platform/category slice. Because `category` is single-label in the primary population, category volume shares sum to exactly **100.00%**.

### Rule 6: Project-Defined Sparsity Flag
Categories representing **< 0.10%** of a platform's total primary SoR volume are flagged with a **project-defined sparsity flag**:
- Flagged categories must NOT be excluded from calculation if the denominator > 0.
- Comparisons involving sparse categories must not be featured as headline cross-platform findings without explicit reporting caveats.
- If a platform/category denominator equals 0, the metric must be reported as **`N/A`** rather than `0.0%`.

### Rule 7: Automation Metric Definitions
Phase 5 will report three distinct automation metrics:
1. **Automated Detection Rate**:
   $$\text{Rate}_{\text{detection}} = \frac{\sum (\text{count} \times [\text{automated\_detection} = \text{'Yes'}])}{\sum \text{count}}$$
2. **Fully Automated Decision Rate**:
   $$\text{Rate}_{\text{decision (fully)}} = \frac{\sum (\text{count} \times [\text{automated\_decision} = \text{'AUTOMATED\_DECISION\_FULLY'}])}{\sum \text{count}}$$
3. **Decision with Any Automation Rate**:
   $$\text{Decision with Any Automation Rate} = \frac{\sum (\text{count where } \text{automated\_decision} \in (\text{'AUTOMATED\_DECISION\_FULLY'}, \text{'AUTOMATED\_DECISION\_PARTIALLY'}))}{\sum \text{count}}$$
*(Both decision metrics must be reported side-by-side to account for Instagram's concentration in `AUTOMATED_DECISION_PARTIALLY`).*

### Rule 8: Handling of Null Automation Values
In the primary population, nulls in `automated_detection` and `automated_decision` are empirically **0.0000%**. Any nulls encountered during processing must be reported explicitly and excluded from numerators.

### Rule 9: Overall vs. Category-Specific Metrics
Platform-wide overall rates must be computed at the platform total level ($\sum \text{count}$ across all SoRs), which mathematically equals the volume-weighted average of category rates. They must NOT be calculated as unweighted averages of category rates.

### Rule 10: Multi-Label Enforcement Action Prevalence (Authoritative Output)
The detailed multi-label **Action Prevalence Table** across the 14 normalized actions is the authoritative Phase 5 enforcement output:
$$\text{Prevalence}(\text{Action}) = \frac{\sum (\text{count where action is present})}{\sum (\text{all SoRs in the platform/category slice})}$$
Action prevalence percentages are independent and are **not expected to sum to 100%**. The mutually exclusive high-level enforcement channel is **OPTIONAL / DEFERRED** and not required for Phase 5 implementation. Assigning an SoR to a single channel requires an analyst-defined priority hierarchy that is not inherent in the DSA schema; no arbitrary priority hierarchy should enter the core processed benchmark unless explicitly approved later for Phase 6/7 visualization needs.

### Rule 11: Exclusion of Supplementary Platform Data
No self-reported supplementary metrics from YouTube or Instagram will be used in Phase 5 quantitative analysis, modeling, or benchmark tables.

### Rule 12: Practical Effect Size Over P-Value Significance
Cross-platform comparisons in Phase 5 and Phase 6 must evaluate the **magnitude** of differences rather than treating statistical significance alone as evidence of an important finding.

Given that the primary dataset represents hundreds of millions of Statements of Reasons, conventional significance tests such as chi-square tests or two-proportion z-tests can produce extremely small p-values for differences that are operationally trivial.

For rate comparisons, report at minimum:
- **Absolute percentage-point difference**

And, where substantively useful:
- **Relative risk / rate ratio**

*Example*:
- Platform A automation rate = 96%
- Platform B automation rate = 95%
- The primary effect size is **+1 percentage point**, not merely a very small p-value resulting from the enormous number of represented SoRs.

**Uncertainty & Significance Reporting Constraints**:
- If inferential confidence intervals are calculated, clearly state what source of uncertainty they are intended to represent.
- Do NOT automatically present conventional sampling confidence intervals as if the DSA aggregate were a random sample of moderation events. The dataset is an administrative record of submitted Statements of Reasons during the defined observation window, and important uncertainty also comes from reporting practices, measurement choices, category usage, and platform behavior.
- If a significance test is performed:
  - It must be reported alongside an effect size.
  - The p-value must never be used as the standalone justification for calling a difference meaningful.
  - Statistical significance must be distinguished from practical / operational significance.

**Headline-Finding Selection Priorities (Phase 6)**:
For Phase 6 headline-finding selection, prioritize:
1. **Effect magnitude**
2. **Consistency** across relevant categories/time periods
3. **Denominator / represented SoR volume**
4. **Robustness** to sparse-category and reporting caveats
5. **Operational relevance** to a Trust & Safety team

A p-value alone is **NOT** sufficient grounds for a headline finding.
